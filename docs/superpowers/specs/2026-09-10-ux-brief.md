- Authority: spec §2 decisions win over this brief; this brief owns copy and section order. Facts only from spec Appendix A.
- Strings in `code` are final and pasted as-is. `{x}` = value from `lib/site.js` or project data. `{n} projects` means `1 project` / `{n} projects` (singular at 1).

60-second path on a 390 px phone (the test every home change must pass):

2. 10–30 s, first scroll: summary (names the fields) and the four Selected projects cards (field label + title + summary + tools).

Rules that follow: no intro animation, splash, or image gating the hero; contact is one tap from the first screen; every project card shows its field(s) and tools so a recruiter can keyword-match without opening it.

## 3. Filter UX (`/projects`)

- Chips in order: `All` · `Analog IC` · `Digital & Verification` · `Embedded & PCB` · `Research`.
- Chip content: `{label}` followed by the count in its own muted span, no brackets. Current values: `All 14`, `Analog IC 3`, `Digital & Verification 5`, `Embedded & PCB 6`, `Research 1`. Counts are fixed totals and do not change with the selection. The SRAM array is in two fields, so the field counts add to 15 while All is 14. This is correct, do not "fix" it.
- State: exactly one chip has `aria-pressed="true"`. Pressing the chip that is already pressed does nothing (no toggling off; `All` is the way back).
- URL: `All` → `/projects` (param removed); a field → `/projects?field={slug}`. `router.replace(..., { scroll: false })`, so chip clicks add no history entries and do not scroll; Back leaves the page, and returning from a project restores the filter. A missing or unknown `field` shows All pressed and leaves the URL as it is. Home field cards and project-page field chips are ordinary links to the filtered URL.
- Keyboard: native `<button>`s in normal tab order (no roving tabindex, no arrow-key handling). Enter or Space activates. Focus stays on the chip that was pressed. Visible focus ring.
- 390 px: chips wrap onto extra lines. No horizontal scroller, because a hidden chip hides a field from the recruiter.
- Result line (visible, inside `aria-live="polite"`, announced only when it changes):
  - All: `Showing all {n} projects`
  - Field: `Showing {n} {label} projects`, e.g. `Showing 6 Embedded & PCB projects`; singular `Showing 1 Research project`
  - Zero: `No projects in this field yet. Choose All to see every project.` (this is the empty state from §2; the grid is not rendered)

## 4. Project page reading order & scannability

Reading order is the §2 `/projects/[slug]` list, and DOM order must match it. No layout may place Overview, Links or a sidebar before Key work in the DOM.

Above the fold:

| Viewport | Must be visible without scrolling (below the sticky header) |
|---|---|
| 390 × 844 | Breadcrumb, h1 (at most 3 lines), field chips, meta line, summary lead. For titles of 2 lines or fewer, the `Key work` heading also starts on the first screen. |
| 1280 × 800 | Everything above, plus all `Key work` bullets and the `Tools & skills` chips. |

Rules:
- Only the breadcrumb comes before the h1. No cover image, banner or decorative block pushes the title down.
- Section headings are the same fixed words on all 14 pages (`Key work`, `Tools & skills`, `Links`, `Files`, `Overview`), so a reader learns the pattern on the first page.
- Key work: 2–5 one-sentence bullets, copied verbatim from Appendix A. Tools are chips, never a prose list.
- The summary lead is one sentence of 160 characters or fewer and never restates the title word for word.
- Links and files show an external/new-tab icon plus the hidden ` (opens in new tab)` suffix.
- Field chips double as a way back to a filtered list. Previous/next lets an engineer page through projects in `order` without going back to the list.

## 5. Audio UX

### In-story player (`section aria-label="Audio narration"`)

| State | What shows | Play/pause button label |
|---|---|---|
| Not started (this story is not the loaded track) | Button `Listen to this story` and tip `Best with headphones`. Nothing else. | — |
| Loading | Full controls plus `Loading…` (`role="status"`); seek disabled until duration is known; time `0:00 / 0:00` | `Pause` |
| Playing | Full controls; time `{m:ss} / {m:ss}`, e.g. `0:23 / 1:51` | `Pause` |
| Paused (including ended) | Full controls; pressing Play after the end starts again from `0:00` | `Play` |
| Error | `Couldn’t load the audio.` plus button `Try again` (`role="alert"`) | `Play` |

- Other control labels: `Back 10 seconds`, `Forward 10 seconds`, seek `Seek` with `aria-valuetext` `{m:ss} of {m:ss}` (e.g. `1:23 of 1:51`), select label `Speed` with options `0.75×` `1×` `1.25×` `1.5×` `1.75×` `2×` (default `1×`), `Mute` / `Unmute`, `Volume`. Mute and volume are hidden below 640 px (the device buttons cover it).
- Only `Listen to this story` starts narration. Nothing autoplays on page load.

### Mini-player (in the audio dock, `role="region" aria-label="Now playing"`)

- Visibility rule: shown if and only if a track is loaded **and** the current path is not `/storybook/{track.slug}`. On that story's own page the inline player takes over the same playback, with no restart.
- From 640 px: title link `{track.title}` → story page, `Play narration` / `Pause narration`, seek `Seek narration` (same `aria-valuetext`), time `{m:ss} / {m:ss}`, `Speed` select (spec §8), `Close player`.
- Below 640 px: play/pause, progress and close only.
- `Close player`: stops and unloads the track; the dock shrinks to the music toggle; background music resumes if it was on; the story's inline player shows `Listen to this story` again.

### Background music toggle

- Accessible name is always `Background music` (also the tooltip). On/off is carried only by `aria-pressed`. A visible text label appears only in the `/storybook` hero; in the dock it is icon-only.
- Starts only from a click, never on load, and nothing is remembered between visits.
- While narration plays, the music pauses but the toggle stays pressed. It resumes when narration pauses, ends, errors or closes. No text changes.
- If the browser refuses to play, the toggle returns to not pressed without a message.
- Dock order, left to right: mini-player, then music toggle. The toggle keeps the same corner position whether or not the mini-player is shown. The footer has enough bottom padding that the dock never covers its links.

### Hero video and reduced motion

- Under `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` the `kashful.mp4` video is not displayed. The frame keeps its paper background, h1, intro and music toggle at the same height, so nothing jumps. No poster image (no new asset).
- The video is decorative: `aria-hidden`, muted, no controls, no sound.
- The video never loops; it stops after 5 s (WCAG 2.2.2).

## 6. Mobile navigation

- Below 768 px: wordmark on the left, `Menu` button on the right (menu icon, X icon while open, at least 44 × 44 px). From 768 px: inline nav, no button.
- Opening shows a full-width panel directly under the sticky header, with the five links stacked (each at least 44 px tall) in nav order and `Storybook` last. The active link has `aria-current="page"` and a visible marker.
- The panel closes on: tapping any link (including the current page's own link, which does not change the route), a route change, Escape (focus returns to the `Menu` button), or tapping `Menu` again.
- The panel is not a modal: no focus trap, no scroll lock, no backdrop. DOM and focus order: skip link → wordmark → `Menu` → panel links.
- Widening past 768 px while the panel is open hides it through the breakpoint; no extra script.

## 7. Content style rules (Task 6: the 14 `content/projects/*.md` files)

**Audience and tone.** Write for a hiring engineer. Keep the register of a plain engineering design report: specific, calm, no selling.

**Person.** No first person in project files: no `I`, `we`, `my`, `our`. Make the design the subject ("The core was verified against…"), or use the verbatim Appendix A highlight wording ("Designed a…").

**Tense.** Past tense for the work done. Present tense only for definitions ("A StrongARM latch is…").

**Facts.**
- Copy from Appendix A exactly: `title`, `fields`, `order`, `featured`, `context`, `period`, `tools`, `highlights`, `links`.
- Numbers allowed only where Appendix A gives them for that project (e.g. `64×8`, `16-entry`, `48 V`, `2.4 GHz`, `five-stage`, `FC03`, `UrbanSound8K`).
- Never add results, measurements, percentages, dates, team sizes, tools, links or files.
- Prose may add definitional context only: what a technique, protocol or component is, and why it is used.
- Expand each acronym at its first use in the overview, e.g. `Universal Verification Methodology (UVM)`.

**Summary** (`summary`, shown on cards and as the meta description):
- One sentence, 160 characters or fewer including the final period.
- Starts with the thing that was built, and names its main technique or tool.
- Does not repeat the title word for word, and has no hype.
- Reference: `Transistor-level two-stage op-amp with Miller compensation and current-mirror biasing, designed and simulated in Cadence Virtuoso.`

**Overview** (Markdown body):
- 2–3 short paragraphs of 2–4 sentences, at most about 80 words each.
- Paragraph 1: what the circuit or system is and why it is built this way (definitional).
- Paragraph 2: what was designed or built, restating the highlights with context.
- Paragraph 3 (optional): how it was simulated, verified or analyzed, only as stated in Appendix A.
- Plain paragraphs only: no headings (the page supplies `Overview`), lists, links, images, bold, code blocks or emoji.

**Mechanics.** Use US spelling as Appendix A does (`characterization`, `modeling`). Put a space before units (`48 V`, `2.4 GHz`). Use `×` in dimensions (`64×8`) and an en dash in ranges.

**Banned words.** passionate, cutting-edge, state-of-the-art, innovative, novel, robust, seamless(ly), leverage, world-class, best-in-class, revolutionary, groundbreaking, game-changing, powerful, blazing, successfully, highly, extremely, very, expert, mastered, efficient, optimized. The one exception is `energy-efficient` when restating the SNN abstract. Also banned: story, stories, storybook, narration, and any grade, phone number, referee or CV-download mention.
